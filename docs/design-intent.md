# Design Intent

## 1. Game title and genre

REFRACT - Tower Defense & Strategy.

## 2. Target player and pitch

For players who like short, tactical mobile sessions and spatial puzzles. REFRACT is a tower defense with no towers. The crystal you defend fires one beam, and you bend, split and bounce it with mirrors so it burns the shadows walking the road, not merely crossing it.

## 3. How to play (controls)

Tap PLAY. The beam is already firing and one tile is marked. Tap it to place a mirror and the beam sweeps into its new route. Tap a placed piece to flip, move or sell it, or drag it. The bottom buttons choose Mirror, Splitter, Reflector and Lamp; CORE upgrades the beam. Nothing starts until you tap START WAVE, so planning is untimed and rearranging is free.

## 4. Core loop

Read the formation named above the board, note which gate it uses, route the beam along road segments in the right direction, start the encounter, watch it burn, spend the gold, repeat. Feedback is on the board: the beam thins and reddens past every shadow, a shield flares when it turns light away, core HP counts what got through. Beat eight encounters to win; lose when the core is spent, or the boss reaches it.

## 5. What is in this prototype

One map with two entrances; four piece types; a six-level core; six run upgrades; six threats including a boss; eight encounters plus a named endless mode with temporary road cuts; hints and help; best score; synthesized audio; one offline build. Not included: extra maps, meta-progression, difficulty options.

## 6. Progression and signature twist

A second gate opens at encounter 3, and what comes through it skips the first sweep, so a network built only across the top misses it. Pieces unlock at encounters 3, 4 and 5. After encounters 2 and 5 the run offers three upgrades from six, which change how you build rather than how hard you hit: trade core power for lamp power or the reverse, widen splitter branches, pierce dense bodies, leave a burn behind a hit, or reward lighting a body from two sides. Threats escalate from motes to runners, shielded Bulwarks, swarms and finally Umbra, which advances behind its shield and drops it halfway down the road.

The signature twist is that light has a direction and so do the things it hits. Every shadow takes a bite out of the beam, so those behind take less. A Bulwark carries its shield on the face it walks towards: light meeting that face is mostly turned away, while light reaching its flank or back lands in full. The same mirrors are right or wrong depending which way everyone faces.

## 7. Future-state vision

A full game of hand-built maps that each teach a new optical piece - prisms, lenses, coloured light and colour-keyed shadows - with daily challenges and a score chase, built on the creation tools.
